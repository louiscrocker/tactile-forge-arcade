    How to play

    1

    Pop out

    You start as one of fifty thousand eggs hanging in strings from the roof of your mum's den on the reef. On a spring night she blows water over the eggs: push any arrow to wiggle free and jet up.

    2

    A paralarva in the plankton

    Swim with the arrows and bump into plankton to eat it. Copepods and krill are food, blue ones glow at night, and baby crabs jump away: chase them! Press E to jet. Plankton is near the top at night and deeper by day. You have no shell to shed: eat and you just grow, twice, until you are big enough to settle.

    3

    Visitors

    A school of herring, a comb jelly, a grey whale, a gull dipping at the surface… They never hurt you: if they get too close you just tumble away.

    4

    Settle: find a den

    Big enough now, follow the arrow to the reef and sink to the bottom. Look under the boulders for a crack that nobody is in (an old bottle will do!), and press Space to squeeze in. Taken cracks have a little octopus peeking out!

    5

    The den years

    Stay in your den and press Space to grab the shrimps that drift past the door. Watch the hiding meter (👁️): out in the open by day it drops. Keep still and your skin matches the ground; if the meter runs out you just flatten and blend until it is safe. When you are full, press Space in your den to sleep. Your skin flickers with colours while you dream, and you wake up bigger!

    6

    Out at night

    Bigger now, go out at night to hunt crabs, clams, scallops and snails. Your empty shells pile up by your door: a midden. A hunter coming? Press E to squirt ink and jet backwards, keep still to blend in, or hide in a crack. When you are full, push down on sand to dig a den under a boulder, then press Space to sleep. The arm you reach with most becomes your favourite. A crab pot? Squeeze in: the crabber lifts the lid and always tips you back.

    7

    A giant

    Grown up, a diver may come to visit: hold still and press Space to say hi with an arm. A wolf eel may get an arm tip: you let it go and it grows back. When autumn comes, crawl down the drop-off to the deep. Push down to dig a den for winter. In spring, crawl back home to the reef.

    8

    Boy or girl

    Boys dig a den under a boulder and wrestle rivals away: tap Space to push, and press E (or ▲) when the ring turns green for a big shove. Girls find a boy's den, take one of their own next door, hang their eggs from the roof and tend them for months (press Space to puff a sunflower star away!), then on a spring night press Space each time the swell glows to blow the babies free.

    Pick "I read to play" and a level (A to E). The buttons show words: tap the one that fits. Read a page at each big step of the life cycle, and read the ⭐ missions to know what to do.

    For the grown-ups: read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. The giant Pacific octopus's life cycle is real, simplified for play: about fifty thousand eggs braided into strings and hung in the den, tended for months and hatched at night; paralarvae drifting in the plankton eating copepods, krill and crab larvae; herring, comb jellies, grey whales and gulls in Puget Sound; settling into a crack, a space under a boulder or a bottle; shrimps and crabs taken from the den; the midden of shells; camouflage by chromatophores and papillae, paling when calm and flushing when excited; ink and the siphon's jet; growth without a shell; night hunting of crabs, clams, scallops and snails; lingcod, wolf eels, harbour seals, sea otters and sixgill sharks as predators; arm tips that regrow; favourite arms; crab pots and the crabber letting an octopus go; divers visiting; seasonal moves to deeper water and back; den contests; the male's special arm and the female's months of egg care. The five looks are real resting colours of one species. Nothing in the game is ever hurt: the mother rests when the babies have gone.
