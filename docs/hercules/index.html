    How to play

    1

    Hatch

    You start as an egg inside a rotting log. When it is ready, push any arrow to wiggle out.

    2

    Eat the log

    Push an arrow into the wood to chew it. Soft brown wood is food, and white fungus wood is extra tasty. Dark heartwood, bark and stones are too hard. Tap anywhere to crawl there.

    3

    Moult and grow

    When your skin gets tight you moult and get bigger: little grub, bigger grub, then a giant grub over 100 grams. The more you eat, the bigger your horn will be!

    4

    Make your room

    Full-grown? Dig down out of the log into the soil. When you are deep enough, press Space again and again to press the walls into a smooth pupal room.

    5

    Pupa to beetle

    Watch the pupa change. A soft, pale beetle comes out. Wait for the shell to harden, then dig up to the forest floor.

    6

    The night forest

    Walk with the arrows. Press Space by fruit to eat it (it gives you energy). Push up at a tree to climb. Press E to fly, and E again to fold your wings and drop.

    7

    So strong

    Press Space by a stick to lift it with your horns. Something may be hiding underneath!

    8

    Horn wrestling

    Another male wants your fruit! Go to him and press Space. Then tap Space fast to push, and press E (or ▲) when the ring turns green to LIFT him. Win three times to be champion. If he flips you, you just flutter down and try again.

    📖

    I read to play

    Pick "I read to play" and a level (A to E). The buttons show words: tap the one that fits. Read a page at each big step of the life cycle, and read the ⭐ missions to know what to do.

Eggs laid in rotting wood, three larval instars with moults, wood-eating grubs helped by gut microbes, fungus-rich wood as better food, larval nutrition setting adult size and horn length, a pressed-soil pupal chamber, complete metamorphosis, a soft pale (teneral) adult that hardens, nocturnal adults feeding on fallen fruit, flight with raised elytra, males wrestling with the thoracic and head horns, hornless females, and wing cases that turn from olive-yellow to black in humid air are all real, simplified for play.
