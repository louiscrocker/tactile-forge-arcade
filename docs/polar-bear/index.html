    POLAR BEARCHILDREN OF THE ICEFrom a snow den to the frozen sea

    Be a polar bear! Born tiny and blind in a snow den in the middle of winter, you drink, grow, and tumble out into the spring snow with your twin. Walk with Mum to the frozen sea: wait by a seal's breathing hole, creep, swim from floe to floe, and wait on land for the ice. Then go out on your own, grow huge… and the whole story begins again.

    How to play

    1

    In the snow den

    You are a newborn cub: tiny, blind and hungry, in a snow den in the middle of winter. Wriggle with the arrows to your mum (she is warm) and press Space to drink her milk. Milk makes you grow. After a while your eyes open: the snow walls glow blue!

    2

    Out into the light

    In March Mum breaks open the den. Press Space by your twin to play-fight: tap Space to push and press E (or ▲) when the ring turns green for a big push. Hold E to stand up and sniff the wind. Face down the snowy slope and press Space to slide on your tummy. When Mum lies down, go to her and drink.

    3

    To the sea ice

    Follow Mum on the long walk to the frozen sea. Press Space at footprints to read them. When a blizzard blows, get behind the big rock and curl up.

    4

    The sea ice

    Go to a seal's breathing hole and press Space to wait: then do not press anything until the seal comes up (it slips away). Near a seal resting on the ice, press Space to creep: hold ▶ while its head is down, and freeze when it looks up. Mum finds a meal: go and eat. In a whiteout, hold E to sniff and let go when the ring is green to find Mum.

    5

    Summer and autumn

    The ice breaks up: swim from floe to floe to the shore. On land, wave to the scientists in their buggy, pounce at lemmings (they always get away) and sniff the wind. In autumn the northern lights come out, and the sea freezes again.

    6

    A yearling, then on your own

    Back on the ice with Mum, try it yourself: your own seal hole, your own careful creep. At two and a half, go off on your own: over a pressure ridge, across a lead, and play-spar with the young males.

    7

    Grown up

    Hold E to sniff out another bear's scent, then press Space to say hi. Boys play-spar with a huge male and spend the summer on the shore. Girls go back to the den hills in autumn and push down on the soft bank to dig a den; then feed the cubs, carry a wanderer home, and stand up with them for their first sniff of the sea wind.

    For the grown-ups: read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. The polar bear's life is real, simplified for play: cubs born in midwinter in a snow den (usually twins, about 600 g, eyes shut), eyes open at about a month, out of the den in March or April at 10–15 kg, a week or two by the den, then the walk to the sea ice; still-hunting at seals' breathing holes and creeping up on resting seals (most hunts fail); feast and famine; swimming; the sea ice breaking up in summer and the bears waiting ashore (as in Hudson Bay), living on their fat; the midnight sun, the northern lights and freeze-up; cubs staying with their mother for about two and a half years; young males play-sparring; a grown bear's spring meeting on the ice; a mother digging her den in autumn, often near where she was born. Clear guard hairs, black skin, thick fat, huge paws and a superb nose. Nothing in the game is ever hurt: seals and lemmings always slip away, a lost play-fight is a roll in the snow, cold means a curl-up and a rest, and meals stay off screen ("a meal Mum found").
