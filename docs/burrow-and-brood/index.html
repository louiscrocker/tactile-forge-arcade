    How to play

    1

    Land and dig

    You are a new queen. After she lands, push an arrow into the dirt to dig. Dig down a little way, then press Space to lay your first eggs.

    2

    Raise the babies

    Eggs hatch into larvae. Go next to a hungry larva (it shows a little bubble) and press Space to feed it. Larvae spin cocoons, and out come the first workers.

    3

    Be a worker

    Now you are the first worker! Climb out and find seeds and crumbs. Space picks food up; carry it down into the nest and press Space to put it in the food room.

    4

    Farm the aphids

    Push up at the foot of a plant to climb. Press Space next to an aphid with a golden drop to sip honeydew, then take it home.

    5

    Chase the ladybug larva

    A ladybug may lay eggs by your aphids. When her spiky larva starts eating them, go and press Space to bite it off the plant.

    6

    Work together

    A beetle is too big for one ant: press E (📣) to call your sisters. Open 🐜 Who does what to see every ant's job and tap a job to ask for more.

    7

    Dirt, sand, clay and rain

    When your jaws are full of dirt, carry it out and it lands on the ant hill. Sand slides, clay is hard, stones cannot be dug. Rain runs into the tunnels: carry the babies to a dry room.

    📖

    I read to play

    Pick "I read to play" and a level (A to E). The buttons show words: tap the one that fits. Read a page when the colony grows, and read the ⭐ missions to know what to do.

    8

    A kingdom

    Watch the colony grow from one queen to thousands. Press F for time-lapse and Z to see the whole nest. At 5,000 ants, winged princesses fly off to start new kingdoms.

    For the grown-ups: read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. The wedding flight, a queen snapping off her wings and founding a sealed chamber alone, legless larvae fed mouth to mouth, silk cocoons, small first workers (nanitics), all-sister workers, division of labour that shifts with the colony's needs, aphid farming for honeydew, the social stomach, recruitment calls and pheromone trails, cooperative transport, spoil heaps, and how sand, clay and water behave in soil are all real, simplified for play. The soil is a real simulation: sand falls to its angle of repose and water drains through sand but pools on clay.
