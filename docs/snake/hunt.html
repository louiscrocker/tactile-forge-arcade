For grown-ups: the common garter snake (Thamnophis sirtalis) lives across most of North America, from Canada to Florida and coast to coast, more widely than any other snake there. It is harmless to people (it may release a smelly musk if handled) and is protected in some places; the San Francisco garter snake is an endangered species. Garter snakes are born live in late summer, spend the winter in shared dens below the frost line, and come out in spring. Coverboards (boards or metal sheets laid on the ground) are how scientists survey snakes: if you look under one, lift it towards you, look quickly, and put it back exactly where it was. Never handle wild snakes, and if you are outside North America, look for your own local reptiles.
