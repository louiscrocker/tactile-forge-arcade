    How to play

    1

    Born in a bag

    Garter snakes do not lay eggs: you are born in late summer, curled up in a thin clear bag, with a whole litter of brothers and sisters. Push any arrow to wiggle out.

    2

    Smell with your tongue

    Press Space to flick your tongue. You smell with it! Snacks you smell start to sparkle: slither to one with the arrows and press Space to eat it.

    3

    Warm up in the sun

    You are cold-blooded. Watch the 🌡️ warmth meter: sunshine warms you, shade, water and night cool you down. Stay still to coil up and bask; a flat rock is best. Only a warm snake can turn its snacks into growing. Too cold? You curl up and wait for the sun. Too hot? Slide into the shade.

    4

    Hide

    Push down at a board, a log, a rock or a leaf pile to slide under it. A hawk's shadow? Hide, or press E to dash. A raccoon at night? Hide, or your stinky musk will send it away. Nothing ever gets hurt.

    5

    Shed your skin

    When you have grown, your skin gets tight and your eyes go cloudy blue. Hide under cover and rest until they clear. Then go to a rock or a log, press Space to rub your nose, and slither forwards to crawl right out of your old skin. Go back and look at it!

    6

    The winter den

    In autumn, flick your tongue to see the big snakes' scent trail and follow it to the crack in the rocky hill. Push down to crawl in, and keep going down, below the frost line. In spring, crawl up and out into the sunshine.

    7

    Boy or girl

    Boys come out of the den first in spring. When a girl comes out, flick your tongue (Space) to stay on her scent trail and slither fast, before the other boys get there. Girls say hello to a boy, then bask all summer to keep the babies warm. In late summer, hide under a log and press Space each time the glow swells to have your litter.

    For the grown-ups: read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. The common garter snake's life is real, simplified for play: live birth in late summer, each baby in a thin membrane; independence from birth; smelling with the tongue and the Jacobson's organ; cold-blooded basking and shade-seeking, and the need to be warm to digest; hiding under boards, logs and rocks (cover objects, which scientists use as "coverboards" to survey snakes); the blue-eye stage before a shed, rubbing the snout and crawling out of the skin inside out; young snakes shedding often; following older snakes' scent trails to a communal winter den (hibernaculum) below the frost line; brumation without food; spring emergence and basking in heaps; males emerging first and following females' scent trails in the spring gathering; females larger than males, basking more while carrying young for about two to three months, and giving birth to a litter in a sheltered spot. Diet is shown only as "snacks". The hawk, heron and raccoon never catch anything; the musk defence is real. The five kinds are real (eastern, red-sided, blue-striped, black and the rare San Francisco garter snake). Nothing in the game is ever hurt.
