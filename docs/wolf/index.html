    WOLFSONG OF THE VALLEYFrom the den to the long howl

    Be a gray wolf! Born blind in a den under a hill, you drink, play and howl your first tiny yip. Follow the pack down the valley in autumn: read the tracks, swim the river, break trail through the snow and run with the pack. Then leave home, find a mate, dig a den of your own… and the whole story begins again.

    How to play

    1

    In the den

    You are a newborn pup: blind, deaf and hungry, in a dark den under a hill. Wriggle with the arrows to your mum (she is warm) and press Space to drink her milk. Milk makes you grow. After a while your eyes open: everything is blue!

    2

    Out of the den

    At three weeks you wobble out into the light. Press Space by a brother or sister to play-fight: tap Space to push and press E (or ▲) when the ring turns green for a big pounce. Hold E to howl your first yip. When a grown-up comes home with a meal, go to it and press Space to eat.

    3

    The summer meadow

    The pack moves you to a meadow. Creep up to grasshoppers and voles and press Space to pounce (they always get away: it is practice). Walk into a coloured wisp and press Space to sniff it. When thunder rolls, get under the big tree.

    4

    Autumn: on the move

    Follow the pack down the valley, one behind another. Press Space at footprints to read them, swim the river (it is cold!), sniff the scent post at the edge of your territory. If the pack goes on ahead in the dark, hold E to howl and let go when the ring is green: the pack will answer and come back.

    5

    Winter

    Deep snow. Walk in the trail the lead wolf breaks. Keep warm (🔥): snow, the river and the night use up your warmth; the den, a tree and the sun bring it back. Too cold? You curl up with your tail over your nose and rest. Near the herd, press Space to test it, then hold ▶ to run with the pack and watch your puff. The herd gets away (it mostly does). The pack finds a meal, feasts and sleeps it off.

    6

    A yearling

    Spring: you are one year old and there are new pups at the den. Play with them and press Space at the den mouth to keep watch. Then it is time to leave: a lone wolf over the ridge, through the tunnel under the road (never on top!), into a new valley.

    7

    A pair, a valley, a den

    Find the other lone wolf and press Space to say hi. Mark the scent posts, then howl together (hold E, let go on green). In spring, push down on the soft hillside to dig a den. Deep enough? Press Space to finish it.

    8

    Boy or girl

    Boys bring meals home (press Space to carry one, Space at the den to give it), bark a nosy raven away, and watch the first pups come out. Girls have the pups in the den, press Space to feed them, carry a wanderer back, and come out for the litter's first howl.

    📖

    I read to play

    Pick "I read to play" and a level (A to E). The buttons show words: tap the one that fits. Read a page at each big step of the life cycle, and read the ⭐ missions to know what to do.

    For the grown-ups: read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. The gray wolf's life is real, simplified for play: pups born in April in a hillside den, blind and deaf, eyes opening at about two weeks (blue, turning amber); out of the den at three weeks; fed with food the grown-ups carry home; a rendezvous site from about eight weeks with a babysitting yearling; travelling with the pack by autumn, reading tracks and scents, swimming rivers, scent-marking the territory's edges and howling to find each other; single file through deep snow with the leader breaking trail; chases of elk and bison that mostly fail; feast and famine; radio collars and the 1995–96 return of wolves to Yellowstone; yearlings babysitting the next litter; dispersal at one to three years, wildlife crossings under roads, a pair for life, a den dug into a dry hillside. The pack is a family led by the parents, not "alphas". Nothing in the game is ever hurt: the herd gets away, pounces miss, a lost play-fight is a roll in the grass, cold means a curl-up and a rest, and food for the pack stays off screen. To install on a tablet, host the folder on any web server (or GitHub Pages) and use "Add to Home Screen".
