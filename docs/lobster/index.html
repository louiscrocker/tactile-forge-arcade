    How to play

    1

    Float free

    You start as one of thousands of eggs glued under your mum's tail, in her den on the cobble bed. On a summer night she fans her tail: push any arrow to wiggle free and swim up.

    2

    A larva in the plankton

    Swim with the arrows and bump into plankton to eat it. Green diatoms are food, blue ones glow at night, and copepods jump away: chase them! Press E to snap your tail and dash. Plankton is near the top at night and deeper by day. Eat to moult: stage I, II, III.

    3

    Visitors

    A school of herring, a comb jelly, a basking shark, a tern dipping at the surface… They never hurt you: if they get too close you just tumble away.

    4

    Stage IV: find a home

    After your third moult you look like a tiny lobster. Follow the arrow to the cobble bed and dive to the bottom. Look under the cobbles for a hole that nobody is in, and press Space to move in. Taken holes have a little lobster peeking out!

    5

    The shelter years

    Stay hidden in your hole and press Space to eat the bits that drift in. Watch the hiding meter (🪨): out in the open by day it drops, faster when your shell is soft. If it runs out you just tuck in tight until it is safe. When you are full, press Space in your hole to moult. Eat your old shell for calcium!

    6

    Out at night

    Bigger now, go out at night to eat mussels, crabs, urchins, snails and kelp. A hunter coming? Press E to flip your tail and shoot backwards, or hide under a cobble. When you are full, push down on sand to dig a den under a cobble, then press Space to moult. The claw you use most becomes your crusher. A trap? Go in: the lobsterman measures you and always puts you back.

    7

    The long walk

    When you are grown up and autumn comes, walk offshore to the deep water. Push down to dig into the mud for winter. In spring, walk back home to the cobble bed.

    8

    Boy or girl

    Boys dig a den under a cobble and box rivals away: tap Space to push, and press E (or ▲) when the ring turns green for a big heave. Girls find a boy's den and moult there where it is safe, carry the eggs under the tail for months, then on a summer night press Space each time the swell glows to fan the larvae free.

    For the grown-ups: read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. The American lobster's life cycle is real, simplified for play: eggs glued under the mother's tail and carried for nine to twelve months; larvae fanned free on summer nights; three larval stages in the plankton, moulting between them; plankton that rise at night and sink by day; herring, comb jellies, basking sharks and terns in the Gulf of Maine; the stage IV postlarva swimming forwards and settling into a free hole in a cobble bed (a real bottleneck); years of shelter-restricted life; about ten moults in the first year, growing about 15 percent each time; eating the old shell; night foraging on mussels, crabs, urchins, snails and kelp; cod, sculpins, striped bass and seals as predators, and the tail-flip escape; the crusher and cutter claws, decided by use; the Maine rules that small lobsters and egg-bearing (V-notched) females always go back; autumn moves offshore to deeper water and spring returns; claw contests for the best shelter; females moulting in a dominant male's shelter. The colour forms are real (blue about one in two million; orange, calico and split rarer). Nothing in the game is ever hurt.
