    How to play

    1

    Out of the sac, and away

    Push the arrows to wiggle out. Climb to the edge of the roof and press Space. The wind takes you: hold ↑ to let out silk and rise, ↓ to pull it in and come down. Where you land is where you live.

    2

    Build, thread by thread

    Press Space to fix your silk. Walk to another spot, on a wall or under a roof, and press Space again. That is a thread, and you can walk on it.

    3

    Drop a line

    On a thread, or under something, hold ↓ to drop on a line. ↓ lets out silk, ↑ climbs, ← → swing. A line that reaches the ground is a sticky gumfoot line: it snaps walking bugs up into the air.

    4

    The web is your eyes

    At night you can not see much. Watch the silk: a thread that hums has something on it. Quick yellow shakes are a bug. A slow green sway is a leaf. Run to the hum!

    5

    Fling, wrap, sip

    Press Space to fling sticky silk. Then wrap: press Space when the ring is green. A snack! When your skin is too tight, hang from a thread and press Space to moult.

    6

    Visitors

    A wasp, a lizard, a mantis, a broom. Hide in your den, or press E to drop and curl up. A grown girl can press Space to show her red hourglass. They always go away.

    7

    His way

    A grown boy stops building. Follow the arrow across the yard to her web, climb a thread, and pluck it: Space on green, soft and steady. Then walk away.

    8

    Her way

    A soft, steady pink hum is a male saying hello: go and pluck back. Then spin the egg sac in your den, round and round with the arrows, and guard it.

    For the grown-ups: look, do not touch. Black widows are real, and they live in yards like this one. They are shy and bite only when pressed or squeezed, but a bite can make a person very sick, and children are affected more. The game teaches what to do: look, do not touch, do not put a hand where you can not see, and tell a grown-up. In the United States, Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. Read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. Ballooning, the tangle web with its den and gumfoot lines, feeling prey by vibration, comb feet, moulting, young that are striped and darken, the tiny striped male, his plucking and web-folding, the round egg sac: all real, simplified for play.
