    Be a moon jelly in a harbour bay! Swim free from your mum's arm as a tiny planula and stick on under a rock ledge. Grow into a polyp, catch food, bud clones of yourself… then in winter turn into a stack of discs and pop free as a baby jellyfish. Pulse through the plankton, grow four rings, ride the current to the summer bloom, and start the next generation.

    How to play

    1

    Swim free

    You start as a tiny baby in a pouch on your mum's frilly arm. When she lets her babies go, push any arrow to wiggle free. Now you are a planula: a tiny hairy oval. It does not eat.

    2

    Stick on, upside down

    Follow the arrow to the rocky ledges (or the dock's crossbeam). Look for a free spot on the shady underside: many are taken by barnacles, sea squirts, sponges and other polyps. Swim up under a free one and press Space.

    3

    The polyp

    You hang upside down. Left and right lean, down stretches out, up pulls in tight. Food drifting by sticks to your tentacles: press Space to fold it into your mouth. Grow 8, then 16 tentacles, then press Space to bud clones of yourself. A sea slug coming? Pull in tight!

    4

    Winter: pop free

    The cold turns you into a stack of discs, like a pile of little plates. Press Space each time the swell glows green to pulse, and pop free as an ephyra: a baby jellyfish shaped like a star.

    5

    Pulse, pulse, pulse

    Hold an arrow and you turn and pulse that way. Swim into plankton to eat it (copepods jump away!). Press E for a big, strong pulse. Plankton is near the top at night and deeper by day.

    6

    A sticky bell

    As a young moon jelly, food sticks to the slime on your bell when you swim through it. Press Space to lick it off with your four arms. A turtle, a sunfish or a gull? They never get you: a big pulse (E) or diving deeper and they miss.

    7

    The summer bloom

    When you have four rings and are fully grown, follow the arrow into the current across the bay: it carries you out to the bloom, where thousands of moon jellies gather.

    8

    Boy or girl

    Boys swim to a girl with a pink heart and press Space: his little cloud drifts to her, and weeks later her babies swim free. Girls swim into a boy's little cloud and press Space. Babies grow in the pouches on her arms; then press Space each time the swell glows to let them go.

    For the grown-ups: read-aloud uses this device's own voices, and the microphone and every link to another website ask a grown-up first. The moon jelly's (Aurelia aurita) life cycle is real, simplified for play: females brood their planula larvae in pouches on the oral arms; the ciliated planula does not feed and settles on shaded hard undersides (rock ledges, docks); the polyp (scyphistoma) grows 4, 8 then 16 tentacles, catches drifting prey and buds clones; cold water in winter triggers strobilation into a stack of discs that are released as eight-armed ephyrae; the medusa catches plankton in mucus on its bell, which the oral arms collect; four horseshoe-shaped gonads show through the bell; leatherback turtles, ocean sunfish and seabirds eat jellies; blooms gather where currents meet; the sexes are separate. Simplified for play: everything is drawn bigger than life, months pass in minutes, and the boy's and girl's parts are shown as a little cloud and a catch. Nothing in the game is ever hurt: every visitor misses, and the sea slug crawls on by. To install on a tablet, host the folder on any web server (or GitHub Pages) and use "Add to Home Screen".
